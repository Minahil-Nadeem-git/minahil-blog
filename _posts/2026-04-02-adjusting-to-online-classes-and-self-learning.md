# 🌐 Adjusting to Online Classes and Self-Learning

## 🎓 The Unexpected Start

During my second semester at UET Lahore, Faisalabad Campus, a significant portion of our classes was conducted online. At first, it sounded convenient. No travelling, no rushing to classrooms, and the flexibility of attending lectures from home.

However, after a few weeks, I realized that online learning came with its own challenges.

Without the university environment around me, it became much easier to postpone tasks, lose focus during lectures, and underestimate the amount of work that was piling up.

What seemed easy in the beginning gradually became difficult to manage.

---

## 💻 Learning Beyond the Classroom

Online classes taught me something important:

> Understanding a topic and studying it independently are two completely different things.

When lectures ended, there was no immediate access to classmates, no discussions in corridors, and no quick help from friends sitting nearby.

If I didn't understand something, I had to search for resources, watch tutorials, read documentation, and spend extra time figuring things out on my own.

Although frustrating at times, this process slowly improved my ability to learn independently.

---

## 📅 When Fridays Became the Busiest Day

As the semester progressed, most classes returned to campus. However, Fridays remained online for some courses.

Ironically, Friday often became the busiest day of the week.

Our online lab sessions involved practical work related to databases and their connection with machine learning concepts. The tasks required much more than simply attending the session. We had to understand the concepts, perform the required work, and prepare submissions within a very limited time.

Many Fridays felt more exhausting than regular on-campus days because of the workload and deadlines attached to the lab activities.

---

## ⚡ A Lesson in Managing Pressure

One experience that I still remember clearly was when we were assigned a substantial amount of work during an online lab session and were instructed to complete and submit it before the end of the day.

Naturally, everyone focused on finishing the task as quickly as possible.

After spending hours completing the work, students began looking for the submission link.

The problem?

There was no submission link available at that time.

The entire situation was stressful because everyone was rushing to meet a deadline while also trying to figure out how the work would actually be submitted.

Looking back, it became one of those memorable university moments that students laugh about later but definitely don't enjoy while experiencing them.

---

## 📚 What Online Learning Taught Me

Despite all the challenges, online classes helped me develop several important skills:

* Learning independently
* Searching for solutions instead of waiting for answers
* Managing deadlines
* Working under pressure
* Adapting to unexpected situations

These skills are rarely taught directly in lectures, but they become essential throughout university life.

---

## 💭 Looking Back

When the semester started, I thought online classes would make life easier.

Instead, they taught me responsibility.

They forced me to become more independent, more organized, and more aware of how quickly work can accumulate when tasks are delayed.

Although the transition between online and physical classes was sometimes hectic, it became an important part of my learning journey and helped me understand that engineering is not only about technical knowledge—it is also about adaptability.
