# 📚 Completing 14 Manuals in Two Days

## 🎉 An Unexpected Eid Surprise

Most students spend Eid holidays relaxing, spending time with family, and taking a break from university work.

I thought I would be doing the same.

On the fourth day of Eid, we received an announcement that completely changed those plans.

We were informed that we needed to prepare **14 complete lab manuals** within a very limited timeframe.

At first, it didn't seem impossible because we already had around seven manuals prepared.

Then we checked the titles.

The problem?

The manual titles we had previously worked on were different from the newly assigned ones.

In other words, much of our previous work could not be used directly.

Suddenly, what looked manageable became a serious challenge.

---

## 😌 The Confidence Before the Storm

Since there were still a few days remaining, many of us believed we had enough time .

There was also another issue competing for our attention.

At the same time, our Digital Logic Design (DLD) project was ongoing, along with assignments, quizzes, and preparation for final examinations.

Like many students, I convinced myself that there was still plenty of time and also we can convince sir to extend the timeline.

So instead of starting immediately, I delayed the work.

That decision would come back to haunt me later.

---

## 🏫 Returning to Campus

When university resumed, many students requested an extension.

The workload was simply too much.

We explained our concerns and hoped that the deadline might be extended significantly.

The response gave us some hope:

> "I will see."

Unfortunately, the next update was not what we expected.

The deadline was extended by only one additional day, and the manual titles would remain unchanged.

At that moment, reality finally hit.

There was no escape.

The manuals had to be completed.

---

## ⚡ The Two-Day Marathon

What followed was probably one of the busiest 48-hour periods of my semester.

For two days, almost everything revolved around manual preparation.

The challenge was not simply writing content.

Every manual had to follow strict formatting requirements:

* Consistent styling
* Proper alignment
* Uniform headings
* Professional formatting
* Correct structure

Even small differences between manuals could result in mark deductions.

As if that wasn't enough, all manuals had to be merged into a single document and prepared for spiral binding.

The workload felt endless.

---

## 🔌 When Technology Decides to Fight Back

Naturally, things became even more difficult.

Just when we needed maximum productivity, we encountered:

* Electricity issues
* Internet problems
* Software formatting challenges
* Printing difficulties

Every time one problem was solved, another appeared.

Simple tasks that should have taken minutes sometimes required hours.

The closer the deadline came, the more stressful everything felt.

---

## 🖨️ The Printing Battle

After finally completing the manuals, another challenge remained.

Printing.

Anyone who has dealt with printing large academic documents knows that the work is not finished when the writing ends.

There were printer issues, formatting concerns, page alignment checks, and binding requirements.

At that point, even finding a functioning printer felt like an achievement.

The final step was spiral binding all manuals together into one professional submission.

By then, everyone was exhausted.

---

## 💭 Looking Back

At the time, the experience felt overwhelming.

Now, it has become one of the most memorable stories of my semester.

The biggest lesson wasn't about formatting manuals.

It was about procrastination.

Had I started earlier, the workload would have been spread across several days instead of being compressed into two exhausting ones.

University has a way of teaching lessons outside the classroom, and this was one of them.

---

## 🎓 What This Experience Taught Me

After surviving those two days, I learned several important lessons:

✅ Start tasks early, even if the deadline seems far away.

✅ University workloads can accumulate very quickly.

✅ Formatting and presentation matter as much as content.

✅ Always expect unexpected technical issues.

✅ Never underestimate a project simply because it seems manageable at first.

Most importantly:

> Future Me should never trust Present Me when Present Me says, "I'll do it later."

---

## 🚀 Final Thoughts

Whenever I see those manuals now, I don't just remember the work.

I remember the deadlines, the formatting battles, the internet issues, the printing chaos, and the panic of trying to finish everything on time.

It was exhausting.

It was stressful.

But it became one of the most unforgettable experiences of my university journey.
